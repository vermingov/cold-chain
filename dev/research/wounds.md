# Wound appearance: stab, incised, chop and gunshot wounds

What real wounds look like on skin, what is visible inside them, how they bleed and how they change over minutes and hours. Written so the game can draw them. Companion to `tissue-mechanics.md` (layer thicknesses, skin pre-tension, gape mechanics, what a scalpel cut shows by depth). Read that file for the layers. This file covers wound types.

Tags: **[S]** stated in a cited source (key in brackets); **[D]** derived by me from sourced numbers (method shown); **[E]** my estimate with no direct source. Treat [D] and [E] as tuning starting points.

Main sources: DiMaio, *Gunshot Wounds*, 2nd ed. (1999), read in full text. Fisher & Petty (eds), *Forensic Pathology: A Handbook for Pathologists* (US DoJ, 1977), with chapters by Stahl (cutting and stabbing), Cleveland (handgun and rifle) and Hirsch (shotgun). Hollerman, Fackler et al. (AJR 1990). forensicmed.co.uk, which summarises Knight, Saukko, Spitz and Mason & Purdue with their primary papers. I could not read Knight's or Spitz & Fisher's own text. Where only a secondary source carries a claim, the claim says so.

## 0. Facts every wound type shares

| Fact | Detail | Tag |
|---|---|---|
| Sharp wounds have no tissue bridges | A blade divides every layer cleanly, so no strands of skin, vessel or nerve cross the wound floor. Blunt lacerations do have bridges, plus undermined, abraded, crushed margins. This is the main visual difference between a cut and a tear. | S [Stahl77], [FM-slash] |
| Skin gape follows tension lines | A cut across Langer's lines gapes into an ellipse. A cut along them stays a slit. Gape needs the full dermis divided. Magnitudes for a 10 cm cut on the abdomen: ~6-10 mm across the lines, ~2-4 mm along them. | S [Stahl77], [FM-stab]; D [TM] |
| Langer's lines are a weak predictor | Hunt (2003) mapped stab victims and found gape often did not align "correctly". Byard (2005) reports cases where tension lines strongly reshaped wounds. Use the lines as the default and add per-wound noise. | S [FM-stab] |
| Inside a fresh wound | Thin white-cream dermis wall (~2 mm), yellow lobulated glistening fat, white glistening fascia, dark-red muscle. Fat lobules bulge into the gap as the skin edges pull back. Colours and per-depth behaviour: [TM] sec. 6-7. | S/D [TM] |
| Subcutaneous fat at exits | Gunshot exit wounds are "lacerated irregular defect[s] with everted margins and subcutaneous fat protrusion". | S [Cleveland77] |
| Vital vs post-mortem | Living: edges swollen, firm, pulled apart (retracted), blood infiltrating the surrounding tissue, clotted blood in the wound. Post-mortem: edges soft, not swollen, lying together, not retracted, no clot. Details in sec. 7. | S [FM-vital] (Hernandez-Cueto 2000) |

## 1. Incised wounds (slash, cut)

Definition: longer on the skin than it is deep. Made by a sharp edge drawn across the skin: knife, razor, glass. [Stahl77], [FM-slash]

### Shape and outline

| Feature | What it looks like | Tag |
|---|---|---|
| Outline | Straight or gently curved line. Across tension lines it opens into a spindle or ellipse, widest at mid-length. Along the lines it stays a near-closed slit. | S [Stahl77]; D [TM] |
| Margins | Clean, smooth, sharply divided, not abraded and not undermined. A blunt edge can leave slightly abraded or bruised margins. Glass gives jagged but still clean-cut edges. | S [Stahl77], [FM-slash] |
| Depth profile (tailing) | Deepest near the start, shallower toward the end, finishing in a superficial scratch ("tail") that shows the direction of the stroke. Suicidal neck cuts are deeper at the start and shallower at the end of the stroke. | S [FM-slash], [Stahl77] |
| Tail length | No measured value. Use 5-20 % of total length as a skin-only scratch, 1-3 mm wide, no gape. | E |
| Hair | Cleanly cut across, not torn. | S [FM-sharp] |
| Bone | Scoring or chipping where the edge reaches bone. | S [FM-sharp] |
| Typical slash length | Volunteers' attack slashes averaged 34 cm diagonally, shoulder to waist. Only 18 % chose a horizontal slash. | S [FM-slash] (Bleetman 2003) |
| Hesitation cuts (self-inflicted) | Several superficial, parallel, partly superimposed cuts beside the deep one: wrists, elbow crease, neck. Present in 62-77 % of suicidal series. | S [Stahl77], [FM-sharp] |
| Defence cuts | Back of the forearms; palm side of the fingers and hand. | S [Stahl77] |

### Inside and bleeding

- Bleeds freely. Vessels are cleanly divided and cut arteries spasm less than torn ones, so incised wounds "often bleed profusely". [FM-slash]
- What shows by depth: see [TM] sec. 7. A cut through dermis shows the cream dermal walls with yellow fat at the floor. Deeper cuts show glistening fascia, then red muscle.
- In a living person, cut muscle fibres retract and pull the deep layer open wider than the skin gape. The pull goes along the fibre direction. No measured value. [E]

### Over time

- Seconds: blood beads along the whole line within a second or two, then fills the gape and runs over the low edge. [E]
- Minutes: clot. Whole-blood clotting in a tube takes about 5-15 min, varying with method. Clot in a wound looks dark red, glossy at first, then matte. [S: FPN-clot range; look E]
- Hours: exposed edges and any clot dry, darken and harden. [E]

### What to draw: incised

- [ ] Line that becomes a spindle across Langer's lines and stays a slit along them (gape 2-10 mm per 10 cm)
- [ ] Clean margins, no abrasion collar, no tissue bridges on the floor
- [ ] Deeper at the start, tapering to a skin-only scratch tail at the end
- [ ] Cream dermis wall, yellow fat lobules bulging in, then fascia and muscle if deep
- [ ] Profuse bleeding along the whole length; blood runs out of the low end
- [ ] Optional: parallel shallow hesitation cuts; defence cuts on the forearm backs and finger palms

## 2. Stab wounds

Definition: deeper than the skin wound is long. Made by a pointed instrument: knife, dagger, bayonet, sword point, ice pick, screwdriver. [Stahl77]

### Shape and outline by weapon

| Weapon / event | Skin wound | Tag |
|---|---|---|
| Single-edged knife | Slit with one sharp, acute end and one blunt end. The blunt end is squared off ("boat-shaped"), slightly rounded or torn, or split into a "fish tail". Hunt (2003) calls the fish tail an artefact of rocking the blade. | S [FM-stab], [Stahl77], [LMB] |
| Double-edged blade (dagger, bayonet) | Spindle-shaped slit tapered to a point at both ends. | S [FM-stab], [ACEP] |
| Double-edged with ricasso, fully driven in | Blunt section by the guard can make both ends look blunt. | S [LMB] |
| Back-sharpened single edge | Spindle when shallow; one end squares off when driven deeper. | S [FM-stab] |
| Withdrawn at a different angle, or the victim moves | "V", "L" or dovetail shape, or a nick: a short incision running off the acute end. | S [FM-stab], [Stahl77], [LMB] |
| Rocked blade | Wound much larger than a straight thrust would leave. | S [FM-stab] |
| Oblique thrust | Longer slit; one edge undercut, the other bevelled. Fat is visible on the bevelled side. | S [FM-stab] |
| Serrated blade | Usually looks like a plain single edge. Sometimes a row of small abrasions where the blade was drawn, often at one end. | S [FM-stab], [LMB] |
| Ice pick / awl | Round hole with a thin abrasion rim; looks like a small-calibre gunshot entry. | S [FM-stab], [ACEP] |
| Phillips screwdriver | Small X or stellate wound. | S [ACEP] |
| Flat screwdriver / chisel | Rectangular, abraded margins. | S [ACEP] |
| Scissors | Closed: one Z-shaped wound. Open: two wounds. | S [ACEP] |
| Fork | Paired holes at the tine spacing. | S [ACEP] |

### Dimensions

| Fact | Value | Tag |
|---|---|---|
| Skin length vs blade width | Skin shrinks back by up to 2 mm after withdrawal, so a straight thrust leaves a slit slightly shorter than the blade width at the depth reached. An oblique thrust or rocking makes it longer. | S [FM-stab], [ACEP] |
| Partial penetration | Length matches only the width of the blade at the deepest point reached, not the blade's full width. | S [FM-stab] |
| Depth vs blade length | Can exceed the blade on compressible areas (chest wall, abdomen): a 5-inch (12.7 cm) blade has left an 8-inch (20 cm) track. | S [LMB], [Stahl77] |
| Edge abrasion | Usually absent. Present only with great force. | S [Stahl77] |
| Hilt / guard mark | Bruise or abrasion along one edge or beside one end, where the guard hit; more likely over the chest. Rare: 3 % of cases (Hunt & Cowling 1991). The assailant's fist can leave a bruise too. | S [FM-stab], [ACEP] |
| Force | Skin perforates at 5-50 N with a sharp point; human stabs reach ~800 N (slashes ~212 N). | S [FM-stab], [FM-slash] |

### Inside and bleeding

- Slit-shaped wounds bleed less externally than their depth suggests. The margins lie close together and tissue elasticity presses the track shut (Levy & Rao 1988). The serious bleeding is internal: chest, abdomen, heart. [FM-stab]
- Most stab deaths are from bleeding. 71 % of victims survived more than 5 minutes (Levy & Rao). [FM-stab]
- Fat may bulge out of a gaping stab, more on the bevelled side of an oblique stab. [FM-stab]; bulge size [E]
- Cheap knife tips can snap off on bone and stay in the wound. [FM-stab], [Stahl77]

### What to draw: stab

- [ ] Slit, length ≈ blade width at depth (−0-2 mm), oriented by the blade, then pulled into a spindle if it lies across Langer's lines
- [ ] End shape from the blade: acute + squared/fishtail (single edge), two acute (double edge)
- [ ] Optional nick or V from twisting or withdrawal; longer slit for oblique or rocked thrusts
- [ ] Rare (3 %) hilt bruise/abrasion beside one end
- [ ] Little external blood relative to depth; a trickle from the low end; heavy bleeding only if a superficial vessel is cut [E]

## 3. Chop wounds (axe, hatchet, machete, cleaver, sword blow)

Definition: a heavy sharp-edged blade swung into the body. They combine sharp and blunt force. [FM-chop], [Stahl77]

### Shape and outline

| Feature | Detail | Tag |
|---|---|---|
| Overall | Deep, gaping, trench-like. Often reaches major vessels, nerves, muscle and bone. | S [Stahl77], [FM-chop] |
| Margins | Sharp, but often abraded and bruised from the blunt mass of the blade. Some have a rim of abrasion from a dull or rusty edge. | S [FM-chop] |
| Axe vs cleaver vs machete | Cleaver: narrow, sharp entry. Axe: more crushed. Machete: in between. Experiments on pig bone (Humphrey & Hutchinson 2001). | S [Hacking] (abstract only) |
| Typical swing | Vertical or oblique, top to bottom. Targets mostly the head and neck, then the trunk. Most victims had more than 5 wounds. Defence chops on hands and forearms, sometimes amputated fingers. | S [FM-chop] (Ong 1999) |
| Example size | Old case of an axe-like blow to the back of the head: ~10 cm long, ~2.5 cm wide. | S [FM-chop] |
| Scalp chop | Straight, smooth-bordered scalp wound over a straight, sharp skull defect along the blow. | S [Nikolic13] |
| Low-energy repeated chops | Parallel superficial grooves 1-2 mm deep in the outer table of the skull; inner table intact. | S [Nikolic13] |

### Bone

- Notched, cut, chipped or shaved; linear fractures radiate from the cut. Heavy blows fragment and comminute bone. [FM-chop]
- Skull: the outer table is often cleanly cut while the inner table fractures, with bone chips driven inward. [FM-chop]
- Limb bone: a cut through bone is always an open fracture, and the skin wound can look small next to the damage underneath. [Machete] (secondary summary)
- "Chattering" (a rough, stepped edge) on bone in about half of axe/hatchet blows; impact surfaces are smooth with striations, fracture surfaces rough (Lynn & Fairgrieve 2009, pig limbs). [Axe09]

### Sword

- No dedicated forensic series found. A sword drawn along the skin leaves a long incised wound (sec. 1); a hard swing behaves as a chop with a cleaner, narrower edge than an axe; a thrust is a double- or single-edged stab (sec. 2). [E from the weapon classes above]

### What to draw: chop

- [ ] Wide, deep, trench-like gape (several times the skin gape of an incised wound, from the blade's wedge) [E]
- [ ] Sharp edges with a thin bruised or abraded band, widest for an axe, slight for a cleaver
- [ ] Bone visible at the floor with a clean notch, chips, radiating crack lines; skull outer table cut, inner table shattered inward
- [ ] Heavy bleeding (large vessels cut cleanly) [S Stahl77 "frequently involving major blood vessels"]
- [ ] Defence chops on the hands and forearms, possible partial finger amputation

## 4. Gunshot wounds: rifled weapons (handgun and rifle)

### 4.1 The entrance hole

| Fact | Value | Tag |
|---|---|---|
| Shape | Round to oval, "punched out". Skin recoils after perforation, so the hole is smaller than the bullet; calibre cannot be read from it. | S [DiMaio], [FM-rifled], [Geisenberger22] |
| Measured size | 9 mm Luger, distant shots into pig skin: 5.6 ± 0.6 mm on the belly, 3.3 ± 0.4 mm on the back. Same gun, same round: a 1.7× difference by body region. | S [Geisenberger22] |
| .22 rimfire (5.6 mm bullet) | ~5 mm including the abrasion ring; 3 mm on stretchy elbow skin. Mistaken for ice-pick wounds and vice versa. | S [DiMaio] |
| Abrasion ring (collar) | Rim of flattened, rubbed-raw epidermis around the hole on almost all entries at any range. Fresh: reddish / reddish-brown, moist, fleshy. Dries brown, then black. | S [DiMaio] |
| Ring width | Varies with calibre, angle and site; wider over the clavicle. DiMaio's worked description: "averages 1 mm wide, except from 3 to 6 o'clock, where it averages 3 mm". | S [DiMaio] |
| Bullet shape | Round-nose bullets leave the largest abrasion ring and the smallest hole; wadcutters the reverse. | S [Pircher17] (abstract) |
| Eccentric ring | Oblique hit: ring wider on the side the bullet came from; angled entries look teardrop-shaped. Body curves (breast, crouching) also make rings eccentric, so the ring alone cannot prove direction. | S [DiMaio], [FM-rifled] |
| Contusion ring | Thin bruise around the entry from bleeding under the skin. | S [FM-rifled] (Thali 2002) |
| Bullet wipe | Grey to black rim of soot and lubricant around the hole in clothing (first layer only), sometimes on skin. Not powder. | S [DiMaio], [FM-rifled] |
| No abrasion ring | Possible with high-velocity jacketed rounds (.357 Magnum, 9 mm, centrefire rifle). Absent as a rule on palms and soles and on axillary re-entries. | S [DiMaio] |
| Micro-tears | High-velocity rifle entries: small (<1 mm) splits radiating all round the hole, with or without an abrasion ring. Barely visible to the naked eye. | S [DiMaio] |
| Palms, soles, elbow | Thick skin: entry is stellate with 1-3 mm radiating tears, or H- or slit-shaped, without an abrasion ring. Looks like an exit or a cut. | S [DiMaio] |
| Over bone at a distance | Head (forehead at the hairline, eyebrow ridges, cheekbone, jaw edge, crown, back of head) and elbow: distant entries can be stellate or irregular and look like contact wounds. | S [DiMaio] |
| Large rifle entries on the chest side | Distant entries between the mid-clavicular and posterior axillary lines can reach 25 mm from a 7.62 mm bullet. Cause unknown. | S [DiMaio] |
| Tail splash | High-velocity rifle: shredded tissue thrown backward out of the entrance; it can land on the inside of clothing around the entry hole (less than at the exit). | S [DiMaio] |

### 4.2 Range of fire (handgun unless noted)

| Range | Skin around the hole | Tag |
|---|---|---|
| Hard contact (muzzle pressed in) | Little outside: a narrow seared, soot-blackened edge that will not wash off. Soot and powder go down the track. Over soft tissue: round hole, possibly a muzzle imprint. Underlying muscle cherry-red from CO. | S [DiMaio] |
| Hard contact over bone (head) | Gas lifts the scalp off the skull and tears it: stellate or cruciform split with blackened seared edges that fit back together into the original hole. Weaker rounds (.32/.380 ACP) give a big round ragged blackened hole. A .22 Short usually does not tear the scalp; a .357 Magnum usually does. Soot on the outer table, sometimes the inner table and dura. | S [DiMaio] |
| Muzzle imprint | Abrasion and bruise outlining the muzzle, sometimes the front sight, recoil spring guide or the second barrel. Can be up to twice the muzzle size: a 13 mm muzzle left a 24 mm imprint on the chest. On the trunk the whole body wall slaps the muzzle, so there is often a wide raw abraded zone without soot. | S [DiMaio] |
| Loose contact | Round hole with a band of soot around it that wipes off easily; a few powder grains in the band. | S [DiMaio] |
| Angled contact | Pear-shaped, round or oval black seared zone, plus a larger faint grey fan of soot. The hole sits at the base of the dark zone; most of the dark zone lies on the far side, pointing where the gun aimed. At sharper angles powder tattoos fan out beyond it. | S [DiMaio] |
| Incomplete contact (long gun to the head) | Strip of seared black skin running from the hole, usually downward. | S [DiMaio] |
| Near contact (< ~10 mm) | Hole inside a wide zone of soot baked onto seared, blackened skin that cannot be wiped off; small clumps of unburnt powder on the edge. Wider than loose contact. When angled, the dark zone lies on the muzzle side. | S [DiMaio] |
| Up to 1-2 cm | Dense dark-black circle of soot with a lighter grey ring. | S [DiMaio] |
| 1-2 cm to 15-25 cm | Soot spreads into a "petal" or blossom pattern, or a dense black centre with a scalloped grey edge. It grows, peaks, then fades out by 15-25 cm. Most handguns leave no soot past 20-30 cm. Longer barrels give a smaller, denser soot ring. | S [DiMaio] |
| Intermediate: tattooing from ~10 mm | Powder tattooing: many reddish-brown to orange-red pinpoint abrasions around the hole. They do not wipe off; soot does. The pattern grows larger and sparser with range. With the muzzle angled, the dense side is the muzzle side. Short barrels give wider, denser patterns. | S [DiMaio] |
| Maximum tattoo range: .38 Spl, 4" barrel | Flake powder 45-60 cm; flattened ball 75-90 cm; spherical ball 90-105 cm (animal tests, so treat as upper limits) | S [DiMaio] Table 5.4 |
| Maximum tattoo range: .22 LR, 2" barrel | Flake 45-60 cm; ball 30-45 cm (very fine marks) | S [DiMaio] Table 5.5 |
| Knight's summary (UK) | < 15 cm: hole, collar, flame burn, burnt hairs, soot, tattoo, unburnt flakes. 15-30 cm: no soot, ± tattoo. > 40-60 cm: hole and collar only. | S [FM-rifled] |
| Distant | Hole and abrasion ring only. Shots from 5, 50 or 500 ft look the same. | S [DiMaio] |
| Tattoo dot look by powder | Ball: abundant, small, round, haemorrhagic. Flake: fewer, larger, more prominent. Cylindrical (rifle): larger, irregular, some linear, sparse. | S [DiMaio] |
| Hair | Burnt hair is rare in practice because the gas blows it away. "Clubbed" melted hair tips with loose contact. | S [DiMaio], [FM-rifled] |
| Revolver cylinder gap | Gun held along the body: a linear, L- or V-shaped grey soot mark from the gap, at right angles to the barrel, with sparse tattooing. | S [DiMaio] |
| Through clothing | Clothing absorbs soot and most flake powder, so a close shot can look distant on the skin. Ball powder passes 1-2 cloth layers (rarely 3). | S [DiMaio] |

### 4.3 Exit wounds

| Fact | Detail | Tag |
|---|---|---|
| General | Larger and more irregular than the entrance. Shapes: stellate, slit-like, crescent, round, or completely irregular. Edges everted. No abrasion ring, soot or tattooing. Fat protrudes. | S [DiMaio], [Cleveland77], [FM-rifled] |
| Why larger | The bullet yaws or tumbles and deforms in tissue, so it presents a bigger area at the exit. | S [DiMaio] |
| Site effect | Lax skin: small slits. Skin tight over bone (scalp): larger, stellate. | S [DiMaio] |
| Can be smaller | Occasionally smaller than the entrance, even smaller than the bullet. | S [DiMaio] |
| Rifle trunk exits | Most ≤ 25 mm; the largest DiMaio saw in the trunk was 75 × 40 mm. | S [DiMaio] |
| Incomplete exit | Sequence: bullet under intact skin → one or two small superficial slit tears over it → skin broken but the bullet bounced back in → full exit. A bullet can sit protruding from its exit. | S [DiMaio] |
| Shored exit | Skin pressed on a wall, floor, chair back, belt, bra or tight clothing at the moment of exit: a broad, irregular band of abrasion; fresh = moist and succulent; can carry the weave of the backing material; when dry it can look like a contact entry. | S [DiMaio] |
| Undeformed high-velocity round through soft tissue | Can leave a rounded exit. After hitting bone, exits can look like knife wounds or blunt lacerations. | S [FM-rifled] |

### 4.4 Grazes, tangential wounds, re-entry, intermediary targets

| Type | Look | Tag |
|---|---|---|
| Graze | Bullet skims the skin: elongated abrasion with no perforation. | S [DiMaio] |
| Tangential | Skin torn open into the fat (a gutter). Entry end: abraded "cap"; exit end: split. Small tears along the margins point the way the bullet moved; tissue piles up at the exit end. | S [DiMaio] |
| Superficial perforating | Entry and exit close together. Entry: complete but eccentric abrasion ring. Exit: abrasion on only part of its edge, pointing onward. Skin over a shallow track can show stretch marks. | S [DiMaio] |
| Very shallow angles | A series of interrupted surface bruises along the path. | S [FM-rifled] |
| Re-entry (e.g. arm into chest) | Large, irregular hole, ragged edges, wide irregular abrasion ring. Re-entry in the armpit: oval to slit-shaped, thin or absent ring, looks like an exit. With the arm pressed against the chest, both the arm exit and the chest entry are shored. | S [DiMaio] |
| After an intermediary target (door, glass, arm) | Larger, irregular hole with a wider, irregular abrasion ring; pseudo-tattooing ("stippling") from glass, wood or bullet fragments; jacket and core can make two entries. | S [DiMaio] |
| Bullet fragment stippling | Larger, more irregular, more haemorrhagic marks than powder tattoos, often with lead in them. | S [DiMaio] |
| Ricochet | Deformed bullet: atypical, irregular entry. | S [DiMaio] |

### 4.5 Rifles (high velocity)

| Fact | Detail | Tag |
|---|---|---|
| Temporary cavity | Up to 11-12.5× the bullet diameter, lasting 5-10 ms. Handgun cavities are small and play little role. | S [DiMaio] |
| Tissue response | Muscle springs back with only a small rim of dead tissue around the track. Liver breaks up to a permanent cavity about the size of the temporary one. Lung barely cavitates. | S [DiMaio] |
| 7.62 mm NATO M80 | Yaw starts after ~15 cm; maximum disruption at ~28 cm. | S [DiMaio] |
| 5.56 mm M193 | Yaw significant at ~12 cm; bullet fragments at 15-25 cm ("lead snowstorm" on x-ray). | S [DiMaio] |
| AK-47 7.62×39 | Peak cavity of the first yaw cycle at ~28 cm, deeper than a limb is thick. A limb hit that misses bone often looks like a handgun wound. AK-74 (5.45 mm) peaks at ~11 cm and wrecks limbs. | S [Hollerman90] |
| Hunting (soft-point) bullets | Expand right after entry: big cavity near the surface, lead fragments throughout the track, organs pulped. | S [DiMaio] |
| Contact to chest/abdomen | Benign outside: round hole, larger than a pistol's, seared edges, less soot than a handgun, muzzle imprint common, skin rarely torn. Inside: heart and liver pulped. | S [DiMaio] |
| Contact to head | Explosive: large parts of skull and brain blown away, scalp torn in flaps, extensive comminuted fractures. A flash suppressor diverts gas and leaves a petal-shaped soot/sear pattern. In the mouth: tears at the mouth corners, nasolabial folds, beside the eyes, along the nose. | S [DiMaio] |
| Distant head entries | Can be stellate (the cavity balloons skin over bone). | S [DiMaio] |
| Rifle tattooing | .30-30 cylindrical powder: heavy at 15 cm, a few marks at 30 cm, none at 45 cm. Ball powder: to 75 cm. .223 ball: heavy at 45 cm, scattered at 90 cm, none at 105 cm. Rifle soot and tattoo ranges are roughly double a handgun's. | S [DiMaio], [FM-rifled] |
| Clothing imprints | The temporary cavity can slap the body wall against clothing or objects, printing their pattern on the skin. | S [DiMaio] |

### 4.6 Skull and bone

- Through bone a bullet bevels outward in its direction of travel. Entry in the skull: round to oval, sharp-edged punched-out hole in the outer table with a larger cone-shaped bevel on the inner table. Exit: the reverse. [DiMaio], [Cleveland77]
- Contact entries can show external bevelling too, so check both tables. [DiMaio]
- Keyhole defect (shallow angle): one end of the hole is sharp-edged like an entry, the other bevelled outward like an exit. [DiMaio], [FM-rifled]
- Gutter wounds (tangential): grade 1 grooves the outer table only; grade 2 also fractures the inner table; grade 3 perforates the middle of the groove with depressed fragments. [DiMaio]
- Lead bullets leave a thin grey lead rim on the bone hole. [DiMaio]
- Bone fragments become secondary missiles. [Cleveland77], [DiMaio]

### 4.7 Bleeding from gunshot wounds

- Real shooting scenes are usually bloody, but some show almost no blood: the bleeding is internal, or clothing soaks it up and acts as a pressure bandage. The only visible blood may be a dime-sized stain on the clothing over the entry. [DiMaio]
- Minimal external bleeding goes with small calibres on clothed, raised (non-dependent) areas. [DiMaio]
- Head wounds usually bleed freely. Exception: a .22 contact entry sealed by hot gas, with no blood at all. [DiMaio]
- Exits are larger, ragged and everted, so they should bleed more freely than the small, tamponaded entry. No measured comparison found. [E]
- Rifle contact wounds of the trunk look small outside while the chest or belly fills with blood. [DiMaio]; volume [E]

### What to draw: handgun and rifle

- [ ] Entry: round/oval hole 3-6 mm for handguns (smaller than the bullet), 1-3 mm abrasion ring (red-brown fresh, browner/black dry), eccentric toward the shooter at an angle
- [ ] Range layer: contact = seared black edge ± muzzle imprint (≤ 2× muzzle) ± stellate split over the skull; near contact = wide black seared zone; to 15-30 cm = soot ring/petals that blood can wash off; to 45-105 cm = red-orange pinpoint tattoo dots that never wash off; beyond = hole + ring only
- [ ] Rifle entry: often no ring, fine micro-tears all round
- [ ] Exit: bigger, irregular (slit, star, crescent, ragged), everted lips, fat poking out, no ring, no soot; shored = broad scraped band
- [ ] Graze = elongated scrape; tangential = torn gutter with edge tears pointing the way the bullet moved
- [ ] Little external blood at a clothed entry; freer flow at exits and head wounds
- [ ] Skull: neat outer hole, wide inner bevel; keyhole for shallow hits

## 5. Shotgun wounds

### 5.1 Entry pattern vs range (birdshot, 12-gauge-class, body skin)

| Range | Wound | Tag |
|---|---|---|
| Contact, trunk | Round hole about the bore size (~18.4 mm for a 12-gauge [D from DiMaio's 0.725 in bore]); seared, blackened edges; no soot outside on hard contact; no skin split. Detailed muzzle imprint, including the unfired second barrel of a double; sometimes the hand, a watch strap or a neck chain is printed. Wide raw abraded zone around the hole. Muscle cherry-red from CO up to ≥ 15 cm away. | S [DiMaio] |
| Contact, head | Bursting wound: skull fragmented, brain pulped and often ejected, scalp torn in large flaps. 12-gauge mouth shots burst from mouth to scalp in 74 %; 20-gauge only 9 % (55 % no tears or only tears around the mouth). Soot on the palate and tongue. | S [DiMaio] (Harruff) |
| Loose/near contact to ~30 cm | Ring of soot around the hole; bigger and fainter with range; gone by ~30 cm. | S [DiMaio] |
| Contact to ~60 cm (2 ft) | Single round hole ~19-25 mm (¾-1 in), any gauge except .410. Contact to 15-30 cm: 2-4 cm with heat-seared edge and marginal abrasion; wad inside the wound. | S [DiMaio], [FM-smooth] |
| ~90 cm (3 ft) | Hole ~22 mm (modified choke) to ~32 mm (cylinder bore); scalloped edges. | S [DiMaio] |
| ~1.2 m (4 ft) | ~25 mm (modified) to ~44 mm (cylinder); scattered satellite pellet holes start. | S [DiMaio] |
| ~1.8-2.1 m (6-7 ft) | Slightly irregular central hole with a definite cuff of satellite holes (modified); ragged hole with a prominent cuff (cylinder). The central mass usually breaks up by ~2 m. | S [DiMaio], [FM-smooth] |
| > 3 m (10 ft) | From an irregular central hole with many satellites down to only separate pellet holes. No central defect by ~3 m. | S [DiMaio], [FM-smooth] |
| Rule of thumb (UK) | Spread in cm ÷ 3 ≈ range in metres. Unreliable: test-firing is the only accurate method. | S [FM-smooth] (Knight) |
| Soot / tattoo / burn | Scorching and singeing end by ~30 cm; soot fades at 20-40 cm; 12-gauge tattooing to 60 cm with flake powder and 75 cm with ball (a few marks at 90 cm); none past ~2 m. | S [FM-smooth], [DiMaio] |
| Pellet hole look | Distant pattern: small round holes, each with an abraded rim, no soot or tattoo. | S [FM-smooth] |

### 5.2 Pattern spread at longer range (Breitenecker 1969)

Spread of the shot pattern (inches converted to cm):

| Choke | 4.6 m (5 yd) | 9.1 m (10 yd) | 13.7 m (15 yd) | 18.3 m (20 yd) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Cylinder | 20 cm | 51 cm | 66 cm | 76 cm |
| Half choke | 13 cm | 30 cm | 41 cm | 51 cm |
| Full choke | 8 cm | 23 cm | 30 cm | 38 cm |

S [Hirsch77]. Ammunition brand changes patterns as much as choke does. Stray "fliers" are ignored when measuring. [DiMaio]

### 5.4 Wads

| Wad mark | Range | Tag |
|---|---|---|
| Wad inside the wound | Close range, while there is one large hole | S [DiMaio] |
| Plastic shot-cup "petal" marks (12/16/20 ga) | Four rectangular abrasions in a Maltese-cross pattern around a round entry, 30-90 cm. Sometimes only one petal mark. Always with powder tattooing on bare skin. | S [DiMaio] |
| .410 petals | Three marks, from 7.5-12.5 cm; widest at 30-52 cm; gone by 60 cm | S [DiMaio] |
| Wad striking the wound edge | Round entry with a symmetric ring plus a big irregular abrasion on one side | S [DiMaio] |
| Separate wad abrasion beside the pattern | Round or oval abrasion, 1.5-2.4 m (5-8 ft); filler wads mark up to 4.6 m, plastic wads up to 6 m | S [DiMaio], [FM-smooth] |

### 5.5 Exits and inside

- Exits are rare except in contact head wounds (ragged, irregular, everted) and close-range shots through a limb. At longer range pellets often lodge just under the skin of the back as palpable bumps. [FM-smooth]
- Temporary cavity of a pellet is cone-shaped, widest at the entrance, because pellets slow fast. [DiMaio]
- Mortality: 85-90 % under 5 m, a few percent beyond 11 m. [FM-smooth]

### What to draw: shotgun

- [ ] Range < 0.6 m: one round hole 2-4 cm with seared black edge, soot ring if not hard contact, muzzle imprint on contact, cherry-red muscle
- [ ] 0.9-1.2 m: hole 2-4.5 cm with scalloped edge, first satellites
- [ ] 1.8-3 m: ragged centre with a cuff of pellet holes, then a pattern of separate 2-5 mm holes, each with a tiny abraded rim; spread from the choke table
- [ ] Wad: Maltese-cross petal marks at 0.3-0.9 m; separate round wad bruise at 1.5-2.4 m
- [ ] Head contact: burst skull with scalp flaps; mouth shots tear the mouth corners

## 6. Bleeding by location and vessel

| Situation | Behaviour | Tag |
|---|---|---|
| Scalp | Bleeds heavily and keeps bleeding. Vessels are tethered open by the galea and dense connective tissue and cannot constrict or retract. Enough to cause shock. | S [SP-scalp], [Elsevier-galea] |
| Arterial | Bright red, spurts in pulses with each heartbeat, loses volume fastest | S [UMS] |
| Arterial spray at a scene | Each beat throws a cluster of stains ("projected" pattern); the clusters trace where the person moved | S [BPA] |
| Venous | Dark red, steady flow without spurts | S [UMS] |
| Capillary | Slow ooze from the cut skin surface | S [UMS] |
| Slit stab wounds | Little external blood: elastic margins tamponade | S [FM-stab] |
| Incised wounds | Profuse: cleanly cut vessels spasm less | S [FM-slash] |
| Gunshot entries | Often little outside (small hole, clothing, internal bleeding); head wounds bleed freely | S [DiMaio] |
| Gravity | Wounds on dependent (lowest) body parts leak more. Blood runs downhill from the low end of a wound and pools under the body. | S [DiMaio] (dependent areas); flow [E] |
| Clotting | Whole blood clots in roughly 5-15 min (method-dependent) | S [FPN-clot] |
| Colour over time | Shed blood turns from bright red to dark brown as oxy-haemoglobin oxidises to met-haemoglobin, then hemichrome (biphasic). Timing depends on conditions. | S [Bremmer11] |
| Drying | Drops dry from the rim inward; a small drop at 20 °C no longer smears after ~60 min (faster when warm). Dried pools flake out in the middle and leave a rim ("skeletonisation"). | S [BPA] (Potente 2012) |
| Wiping | Blood wipes soot off a wound; it cannot wipe off powder tattooing. | S [DiMaio] |

### What to draw: bleeding

- [ ] Choose a source per wound: arterial (bright, pulsed spurts), venous (dark, steady), capillary (ooze)
- [ ] Scalp and face wounds bleed fast and long; slit stabs and clothed small entries bleed little outside
- [ ] Blood follows gravity off the low end, drips, pools; clot forms in ~5-15 min, then darkens and browns over hours; pools dry from the rim inward

## 7. Clothing

| Wound | Fabric | Tag |
|---|---|---|
| Contact gunshot, cotton, .38 and up | Cruciform (X-shaped) tear; a .38 Special jacketed hollow-point made a 9 × 8 cm tear. Tears even at 0.5 cm from the muzzle. | S [DiMaio] |
| Contact gunshot, 100 % synthetic | Melted "burn hole", round with scalloped edges, 4-5.5 cm (.38); ~10 mm for .22 LR | S [DiMaio] |
| .22 contact, cotton | Single tear or partial cross | S [DiMaio] |
| Any bullet hole | Bullet wipe: grey to black ring on the first layer the bullet passed through | S [DiMaio] |
| Fibre direction | Fibres bend in the direction of travel, though not all of them do | S [DiMaio] |
| Exit through cloth | Tissue fragments on the inner surface around the hole (also a little at rifle entries) | S [DiMaio] |
| Soot and powder | Show on cloth: soot ring or petals, embedded grains, revolver cylinder-gap strip; synthetics can burn through at the gap | S [DiMaio] |
| Bleeding onto clothes | A dime-sized stain over a small entry, or nothing on the outer layer when inner layers soak it up | S [DiMaio] |
| Stabs and cuts | Clean slit in the cloth matching the blade; large knife tips can catch on fabric. A slit in clothes over a "self-inflicted" stab points to homicide. | S [FM-stab], [Stahl77] |
| Hole vs wound alignment | Holes in loose clothing need not line up with the skin wound; the offset shows the body position | S [DiMaio], [Stahl77] |

### What to draw: clothing

- [ ] Small round bullet hole with a dark wipe ring; contact = X tear (cotton) or melted round hole (synthetic)
- [ ] Blood wicks outward from the hole into a roughly round stain that grows over time [E for the shape]; outer layers may stay clean
- [ ] Clean slit for knives; offset from the skin wound if the cloth was loose

## 8. Ante-mortem vs post-mortem wounds (what a player could see)

| Feature | Inflicted in life | Inflicted after death | Tag |
|---|---|---|---|
| Edges | Swollen, firm, pulled apart by retraction | Soft, not swollen, lying together, not retracted | S [FM-vital] |
| Surrounding tissue | Blood infiltrating the tissue around the wound | No bleeding into the tissue | S [FM-vital] |
| In the wound | Clotted blood | No clot | S [FM-vital] |
| Caveat | Pig experiments: post-mortem incised wounds still showed haemorrhage along their edges, but never fibrin. Bleeding at the edge alone does not prove life. | | S [PigPM17] |
| Powder tattoo dots | Reddish-brown to orange-red | Moist grey or yellow, fewer at the same range | S [DiMaio] |
| Abrasions (collar etc.) | Red-brown | Pale, dry, yellow-brown "parchment" look [E: standard teaching, no source read] | E |
| Blood flow | Arterial pressure: spurts, pours | No pressure: dark liquid seeps by gravity, more in dependent (livid) areas; post-mortem blood often stays liquid (fibrinolysis) | S [TM] sec. 6; seep [E] |
| Window | About 4-6 h around death when vital and post-mortem origin cannot be told apart (histology) | | S [FM-vital] (Raekallio), search set |
| Dried edges | Drying blackens wound edges and can mimic soot or searing; subcutaneous bleeding at an entry can look purple-black like soot | | S [DiMaio] |

### What to draw: post-mortem wounds

- [ ] No gape from retraction beyond the passive skin tension (edges sit closer together), no clot, no red halo
- [ ] Dark, slow seep only, mainly from wounds in livid (lowest) areas
- [ ] Pale or yellowish abraded margins; grey/yellow tattoo dots if shot after death

## 9. Time course of a fresh wound (living victim)

| Time | Change | Tag |
|---|---|---|
| 0-1 s | Skin edges spring apart to their gape; fat bulges in; blood beads along incised edges | D [TM] gape; bead [E] |
| Seconds to 1 min | Wound fills and overflows at the low end; arterial cuts spurt in pulses; scalp keeps running | S [UMS], [SP-scalp]; fill [E] |
| 3-15 min | Clotting; soot can be washed away by the bleeding; tattooing stays | S [FPN-clot], [DiMaio] |
| Minutes to hours | Abrasion collar dries from moist red to brown, then black; wound edges dry and darken | S [DiMaio] |
| ~1 h | Small blood drops dry (20 °C) | S [BPA] |
| Hours to days | Shed blood browns (met-Hb → hemichrome) | S [Bremmer11] |
| 15-30 min onward (microscopic only) | Earliest neutrophils at the margins; nothing visible before ~4 h | S [FM-vital] |
